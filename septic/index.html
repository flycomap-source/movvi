Septic Smells, Slow Drains or Soggy Yard? Read This Before You Call for a Pump-Out

More than 1 in 5 U.S. homes rely on a septic tank. Most problems start the same way: the good bacteria that break down waste get weaker. Here's what to look for and what you can do about it.

5 warning signs your septic system is struggling

  • Rotten-egg smell near drains, the tank lid or the drain field.
  • Slow drains or gurgling pipes, especially in the lowest part of the house.
  • Unusually green or soggy grass over the drain field.
  • Backups in toilets, tubs or the washing machine drain.
  • Needing to pump more often than every 3–5 years.
Important: sewage backing up into the house, or standing wastewater in the yard, needs a licensed septic professional right away. A treatment is for maintenance, not emergencies.

Why it happens: the bacteria balance

Your tank works because bacteria digest solids, grease and paper. Everyday products kill or weaken those bacteria:

  • Bleach, antibacterial soaps and harsh drain cleaners
  • Medications and some laundry additives
  • Grease, oils and food scraps from the garbage disposal
  • “Flushable” wipes, which don't break down like toilet paper

When the bacteria fall behind, solids build up, the drain field clogs and smells appear.

Do septic bacteria treatments work?

Bacteria treatments add live microbes and enzymes to help the tank digest waste, especially in homes that use cleaning products, medications or a garbage disposal. They are not a substitute for pumping, but many homeowners use them as part of regular maintenance to reduce odors and buildup.
